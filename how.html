How it's made

Studied from real battle clips, then drawn from scratch by code.

the process

  1. Watch. Eight top-down pixel monster-battle clips were stepped through at 15 fps and 133 distinct effects were catalogued: phases and frame counts, shapes, palettes, flashes, hit-stop, shake, unit reactions, UI. The clips are cited below as timestamps only; no frame, sprite or colour sheet from them is in this pack.
  2. Distil. What makes those effects read was written down as rules (below).
  3. Draw by code. src/ is a small Python + Pillow generator: a pixel canvas with whole-pixel primitives (discs, rings, squashed ellipses, polylines, pointed shards, a 3×5 font), ~20 archetypes (orb, beam, burst, explosion, splash, debris, pillar, vortex, pulse rings, cloud, spikes, cracks, swarm, aura…) and one table that turns archetype × element ramp × timing into effects. python3 src/build.py rebuilds every sheet, GIF and JSON in under a minute, byte-identical each run. Every effect paints two layers: crisp colour pixels and a soft additive light layer. Since art pass 4 each effect is its own drawing, designed from the move it stands for (a water jet is a stream with foam, Razor Leaf is real leaf shapes, Rock Slide is faceted boulders falling), and substances are split into emissive (fire, lightning, energy: they light the scene) and material (water, rock, leaves, metal, goo, dust: no glow, only small glints where they hit).
  4. Look and fix. Each effect was rendered to a frame contact sheet, looked at, and the archetype adjusted.

the rules

  1. Effects are 2–10× the unit. Units are ~28 px; impacts are 64–96 px, beams and areas 128 px wide. Scale contrast alone says "powerful".
  2. Telegraph slow, release fast. Big moves gather for 6+ frames, then snap out in 1–3. The snap sells the hit.
  3. Frame 0 of a hit is the hottest colour, then every particle walks down its ramp as it ages. No alpha fades: colour does the fading.
  4. Thin strokes, solid props. Rings, cracks and beam edges are 1–2 px; rocks and spikes get a 1 px outline in the darkest ramp step; energy gets a dithered halo behind it instead.
  5. Squashed ellipses fake 3D. Ground rings are squashed to 0.3–0.45; tornadoes are stacks of growing squashed rings with orbiting bits.
  6. The body sells the hit. Hit-stop (40–120 ms), a few pixels of shake, two white frames and a knockback on the target do more than a bigger sprite.
  7. Shape first, colour second. Fire is flame tongues, water is a stream with foam, electric is a zig-zag redrawn every frame, ice is faceted crystals, psychic is wavy ripples, a punch is a glove and a comic star.
  8. Materials don't glow. Water, rock, leaves, metal and goo are shaded from the top-left with a dark outline and get light only as a glint at contact; only fire, lightning and energy light the scene.
  9. Pixel hygiene. Whole pixels only, alpha 0 or 255, dither instead of transparency, frame sizes in multiples of 32, directional effects face right.

layering

ground (decals, shock rings, cracks) → units → front (auras, impacts, projectiles, beams) → screen (cut-in plate, flash, numbers). Inside a frame: dithered halo → body → highlight → core → sparks.

using the files

  • fx/<id>.png — transparent sprite sheet, frames left to right then top to bottom (sheet.columns × sheet.rows).
  • fx/<id>.json — frame size, count, fps, loop, anchor (px inside a frame where the effect attaches: projectile head, beam origin, impact centre, or unit feet), layer, palette, battle timing.
  • fx/<id>.gif — preview at 2× over our backdrop. fx.json — everything in one file.
